Draughts (Checkers)

This is English draughts, called checkers or American checkers in the United States. Each player starts with 12 pieces, and Black moves first.

Goal

Win by capturing all the opposing pieces, or by leaving your opponent with no legal move.

Ordinary pieces

Pieces move only on the dark squares. An ordinary piece, or man, moves one square diagonally forwards into an empty square. Black moves towards White's starting side; White moves towards Black's.

Captures

Jump diagonally forwards over an adjacent opposing piece into the empty square immediately beyond it. The jumped piece is captured. If any capture is available, you must take one.

If the same piece can jump again, it must continue in the same turn. When there is a choice of capture routes, you may choose any complete route; it need not capture the greatest number of pieces. Green rings show the next possible landing squares.

Kings

A man reaching the farthest row is crowned as a king. Its turn ends when it is crowned. From its next turn, a king may move or capture one square or one jump diagonally forwards or backwards.

Playing here

In an online human game, the creator plays Black and shares the four-character code; the player who joins is White. Choose Free Play for two people sharing one phone, or to explore both sides yourself. Against Cassie, choose Easy, Medium or Hard and then choose your colour. Reset leaves the current game and restores all settings.

This version declares a draw after the same position occurs three times, or after 80 turns without a capture or a crowning.

Tips

Keep pieces connected so they protect one another, be wary of compulsory captures that lead into traps, and do not hurry to empty your back row: it helps prevent an early enemy king.
