Nine Men's Morris

Nine Men's Morris, also called Mill or Merels, is played on the 24 points where the board's lines meet. Each player has nine pieces. Black moves first in this edition.

Goal

Win by reducing your opponent to two pieces, or by leaving them unable to make a legal move after all pieces have been placed.

1. Placing

Players alternate placing one piece on any empty point. If your three pieces fill one straight board line, they form a mill. Every time you form a mill, you must remove one opposing piece.

Removing a piece

You must take an opposing piece that is not in a mill if one is available. If every opposing piece is in a mill, any one of them may be taken. Red rings show the pieces you may remove.

2. Moving and flying

After all 18 pieces have been placed, move one piece per turn along a board line to an adjacent empty point. When you have only three pieces left, you may fly: move to any empty point. Making a new mill still permits one removal.

Playing here

Green dots show legal destinations. Blue marks your selected piece. Yellow marks the previous move, and a red-and-yellow empty ring marks a captured piece.

This edition uses the three-piece flying rule. It declares a draw after the same completed position occurs three times, or after 100 consecutive movement turns without a capture.

Tips

Junctions with four connecting lines are flexible. Look for two-piece lines with an empty third point, and especially for positions where one move opens one mill while closing another. During placement, blocking an immediate enemy mill can be as important as building your own.
