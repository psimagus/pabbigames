Chess

Chess is a two-player strategy game. White moves first. Each player tries to checkmate the opposing King: attack it so that no legal move can save it.

How the pieces move

King: one square in any direction. It may never move into check.
Queen: any distance vertically, horizontally or diagonally.
Rook: any distance vertically or horizontally.
Bishop: any distance diagonally.
Knight: in an L-shape—two squares one way and one across. Knights may jump over pieces.
Pawn: one square forward, or two from its starting square if unobstructed; it captures one square diagonally forward.

Check and checkmate

If your King is attacked, it is in check and you must remove the threat. If no legal move can do so, it is checkmate and the game is over. If a player has no legal move but is not in check, the game is a draw by stalemate.

Special moves

Castling: move the King two squares towards an unmoved Rook; the Rook crosses to the other side. The squares between must be empty, and the King may not castle out of, through or into check.
En passant: immediately after an opposing pawn advances two squares past your pawn's capture square, your pawn may capture it as though it had advanced only one.
Promotion: a pawn reaching the farthest rank becomes a Queen, Rook, Bishop or Knight.

Draws

The program recognises stalemate, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule and positions with insufficient mating material.
