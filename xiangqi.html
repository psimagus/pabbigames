Xiangqi 象棋

Xiangqi (roughly “shyang-chee”) is usually called Chinese chess in English. Red moves first. Win by leaving the opposing General with no legal move; unlike Western chess, stalemate is also a win for the player who causes it.

The board and Generals

Pieces stand on the line intersections. Each General—Red 帥, Black 將—moves one point horizontally or vertically and must remain inside its nine-point palace. The two Generals may never face one another on an open file with no piece between them.

How the pieces move

Advisor 仕/士: one point diagonally, always inside its palace.
Elephant 相/象: exactly two points diagonally. It cannot cross the river or jump a piece blocking the diagonal midpoint (“elephant's eye”).
Horse 傌/馬: one point straight and then one diagonally outward. It cannot jump; a piece on the first straight point blocks it (“the horse's leg”).
Chariot 俥/車: any distance horizontally or vertically without jumping.
Cannon 炮/砲: moves like a Chariot when not capturing. To capture, it must jump over exactly one intervening piece—the screen—and land on the next occupied point.
Soldier 兵/卒: one point straight forward. After crossing the river it may instead move one point sideways, but it never moves backwards.

Check, mate and repetition

A General under attack is in check and must be made safe. Checkmate wins, and a player with no legal move also loses. Formal Xiangqi has detailed rules for perpetual checking and chasing; for straightforward family play, this program declares a draw when the same position occurs three times.

Playing here

Tap a piece, then a highlighted destination. The previous move remains marked in yellow: a circle at the starting point and a glow around the piece at its destination. When a General is in check, a green arrow runs from every checking piece to the General, and the checking piece glows green. In a human game, the creator plays Red and shares the four-character code; the player who joins is Black. Choose Free Play for two people sharing one phone, or to explore both sides yourself; Red moves first and the board remains in a fixed orientation.
